Biographical Sketch

A brief overview of my education and career. Click here for my CV.

Originally from East Kilbride in Scotland, Graham graduated from the University of Glasgow with an MSci (Hons.) degree in Physics and Astronomy in 2012. He then obtained his PhD in Astrophysics from the University of Glasgow's Astronomy and Astrophysics group in 2017, supervised by Prof. Lyndsay Fletcher. The topic of his PhD was "Observations and modelling of the chromosphere during solar flares". In his thesis he explored the characeristics of energy and radiation transport through the solar atmosphere, including exploring the white light solar flares, chromspheric spectroscopy, and the role of Alfven waves in flares. Upon completion of his PhD, Graham became a NASA Postdoctoral Program (NPP) Fellow at NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center in 2017, advised by Dr. Joel Allred on the modelling of solar flares. During this fellowship, he continued research into understanding the energy, radiation, and mass transport during of solar flares, with an emphasis on exploring how we can improve the ways in which he model flares and perform model-data comparisons. After the conclusion of his NPP Fellowship, Graham was appointed as a Research Scientist at the Catholic University of America. In that role he continued to be based at NASA GSFC as a co-operative scientist, funded via several NASA grants and the PHaSER co-operative agreement. From January 2026 Graham will return to the University of Glasgow as a Royal Society University Research Fellow (RS/URF). His fellowship will focus on how models might explain recent high-resolution flare observations, where those models fail to stand up to the scrutiny of observations, and where we need to improve those models based on the constraints of observations.

Graham is an expert in the study of the solar atmosphere's response to solar flares, achieved via state-of-the-art radiation hydrodynamical modelling of the solar atmosphere and the analysis of data from NASA observatories. He has published over a dozen papers in peer-reviewed journals, has been invited to present his research at both national and international conferences, and has been awarded millions of dollars in competitive NASA and NSF funding. He is a member of the science team for the MUSE Heliophysics MIDEX mission, scheduled for launch in 2027, and is on the science team for various mission concepts. Graham has been a member of several International Space Science Institute (ISSI) research teams, including as co-leader of an ISSI team with Vanessa Polito focussed on the comparison of solar flare models.

In addition to his research Graham is active in community leadership and advocacy efforts, in the public outreach of science, and in DEIA efforts.
